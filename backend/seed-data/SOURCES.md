## Caveats

- All coordinates are estimates from general geographic knowledge (coordinatesAreEstimated=true for every site); none was taken from a cited source. Offshore field positions (Nasr, Umm Lulu, SARB, Belbazem, Hail and Ghasha) and the southern onshore fields (Qusahwira, Sahil, Rumaitha) are the least certain and should be checked against a map before use.
- ADNOC Gas train count conflicts: 2025 integrated report says 30 gas processing trains, the website says 34. Pipeline length conflicts: about 3,300 km (report headline) vs 3,260 km (website); MEED cites 3,200-3,250 km.
- ADNOC Gas says it has 'eight sites' but does not list them in the text extracted; the seven sites plotted (Habshan, Habshan 5, Bab, Asab, Buhasa, Ruwais, Das Island) plus the pipelines/Taweelah division is an inference, not a published list.
- Maqta and Thamama could not be verified as distinct ADNOC Gas sites: Maqta appears only as the Maqta-Taweelah pipeline, and no 'Thamama' plant was found. The sales-gas pipeline network, IGD/IGD-E2 pipelines, Estidama and ADCOP are linear assets and are recorded in descriptions and figures, not as point sites.
- Abu Dhabi (Umm Al Nar) Refinery, 85,000 bpd, is closed: ADNOC says it was shut down in 2022 (Wood Mackenzie reported the closure in January 2022). It is excluded from sites because the status enum has no 'closed' value. Source: https://adnoc.ae/en/our-people-our-stories/fuel-and-family
- Ruwais refinery capacity differs by source: 817,000, 837,000, 840,000 and 922,000 bpd are all cited. Completion of the Crude Flexibility Project was not confirmed by any source found (latest: more than 85% complete in 2021 with ramp-up expected by 2023).
- Borouge 4 status is ambiguous: Argus reported polymer production from early April 2026, while a November 2026 update said one of its two polyethylene plants (PE7) had only just reached mechanical completion. Marked OPERATING (partial, ramping up).
- Field capacity figures for Bab, Bu Hasa, Asab, Lower Zakum, Umm Shaif, Nasr and SARB/Umm Lulu come from trade press dated roughly 2016-2021 and may be out of date. The adnoc.ae Onshore and Offshore pages returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch, so their content was confirmed only through search-result excerpts. Mender, Shanayel and the Shah oil field are documented as ADNOC Onshore fields but not plotted; Arzanah Island and Dalma could not be verified in enough detail to include.
- MERAM is marked UNDER_CONSTRUCTION although first gas was reported in November 2026 (commissioning under way). RGD Phase 2/3 (Habshan 7 train, Ruwais NGL Train 5) and Bab Gas Cap were pre-FID in the 2025 integrated report; later press reports say US$8.2 billion of RGD contracts were awarded in August 2026, and Bab Gas Cap's post-report FID status was not verified.
- ADNOC Industrial Gases: trade press describes it as an ADNOC 51% / Linde 49% joint venture, while ADNOC Gas's integrated report lists ADNOC Industrial Gases (AIG) among its own businesses and mentions an AIG Mirfa site; the current ownership structure and the Mirfa plant details were not verified. Fertiglobe's current ADNOC shareholding was not verified.
- ADCOP capacity is cited as 1.5 million bpd by some sources and up to 1.8 million bpd by others. Al Reyadah's CO2 pipeline is given as 42 km or 43 km, and one later article says the CO2 goes to Habshan instead of Rumaitha/Bab.
